## Editorial model for the website

Use two levels of case content:

1. **Homepage card**: a short title, three capability tags, and one outcome-oriented sentence.
2. **Full case study**: situation, Russell's role, approach, work delivered, effect/evidence, and attribution.

The homepage cards should show a coherent range of capabilities, not read like an indiscriminate client list. Full cases should explain how Russell thinks and works, while making his personal contribution distinct from the work of the wider team.

## Standards for claims and writing

Every case should answer six questions:

1. **Situation:** What was happening before the engagement?
2. **Objective:** What needed to change?
3. **Personal role:** What was Russell specifically responsible for?
4. **Approach:** What analysis, design, management or implementation work did he perform?
5. **Delivery:** What actually existed at the end (strategy, architecture, prototype, implemented service, campaign, operating process, or tool)?
6. **Effect:** What documented change followed, and how certain is the causal link?

Use precise verbs:

- **analysed, mapped, designed, recommended, facilitated** for advisory/design work;
- **led, managed, coordinated** for leadership and project work;
- **implemented, launched, integrated, automated** only when the evidence shows that the work went live;
- **contributed to** where the outcome was produced by a wider team and Russell's responsibility was narrower.

Avoid converting an intention into an outcome. For example:

- safe: “Designed process flows as the foundation for later automation.”
- unsafe without evidence: “Automated the workflow.”

Similarly, do not turn correlation into personal attribution. Use exact metrics only when the source, time period and meaning are clear.

## Using former-employer work as evidence

The practical conclusion from the discussion was:

> Russell can use his experience, but must distinguish his personal contribution from intellectual property, confidential information and work product belonging to the former employer or client.

### Generally safer to publish

- a client name when the relationship was already public;
- a high-level description of the problem and engagement;
- Russell's accurately described role and contribution;
- general methods and capabilities acquired through the work;
- publicly launched websites, services or products;
- results already published by the client or former employer;
- a newly written summary in Russell's own words.

### Do not publish without permission or a clear public source

- former-employer proposals, decks, reports, designs, diagrams or source files;
- screenshots or creative assets owned by the employer/client;
- budgets, pricing, commercial terms, staffing or contracts;
- unpublished performance data or business results;
- detailed security, data or system architecture;
- customer, employee or research-participant personal data;
- private quotations or testimonials;
- client logos used in a way that may imply endorsement;
- claims that Russell alone produced work delivered by a multidisciplinary team.

Norwegian trade-secret protection generally concerns information that is not commonly known, derives value from being secret and is subject to efforts to keep it secret. General personal experience and skills are distinct from protected confidential material. See [Patentstyret's overview of trade secrets](https://www.patentstyret.no/immaterielle-rettigheter/forretningshemmeligheter) and the [Norwegian Trade Secrets Act](https://lovdata.no/lov/2020-03-27-15).

Copyright is a separate issue: describing one's role is not the same as republishing the deliverables. Rights in work created in employment may belong or transfer to the employer to the extent required by the employment relationship and applicable agreements. See the [Norwegian government's discussion of copyright in employment](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/prop.-104-l-20162017/id2547943/?ch=6).

Personal data and identifiable photographs require separate care. See [Datatilsynet's guidance on publishing employee information](https://www.datatilsynet.no/regelverk-og-verktoy/sporsmal-svar/arbeidsliv/kan-arbeidsgiveren-publisere-personopplysninger-om-ansatte-pa-internett/).

This is practical editorial guidance, not a legal opinion. Russell should review his employment agreement, NDA and relevant client terms, and obtain legal advice if a high-value or disputed case depends on an uncertain right.

## Publication risk system

Apply this classification sentence by sentence:

### Green: suitable for drafting

- publicly known employer/client relationship;
- high-level challenge and method;
- Russell's accurately attributed personal role;
- public product or service;
- public, cited result;
- fresh wording and no copied assets.

### Amber: permission or verification needed

- metrics found only in internal material;
- architecture diagrams and implementation details;
- screenshots, logos and quotations;
- claims that blur strategy/design with implementation;
- vendor selection, pricing or internal process information;
- facts that may be true but cannot yet be sourced.

### Red: remove or anonymize

- information marked confidential;
- contracts, credentials, personal data and commercial terms;
- unpublished customer or employee research;
- source files and internal presentations;
- inaccurate sole-authorship claims;
- any detail prohibited by contract or NDA.

If approval is unavailable, anonymize the case, for example **National tourism organisation**, **Nordic building-materials distributor**, or **US insurance provider**, and remove identifying details that would make the anonymity cosmetic.
